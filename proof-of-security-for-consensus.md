# Proof of security for consensus

In cryptography you have these concepts of security definitions, security assumptions, and proofs of security. A definition allows you to define what constitutes a break in your scheme and the power level of adversaries that it can protect against. Security assumptions are the assumptions you make about things you won't question further, like axioms for your proof. A proof of security then is the logical chain of assertions worked from the assumptions that prove a cryptographic scheme secure. For algorithms built for applications, the best proofs use the fewest most reasonable assumptions while the weaker proofs generally introduce new ones.

I bring this up because I've been seeing a lot of new schemes for Bitcoin layer 2s and want an easy way to classify them. For me, one of the most important aspects to classify on are security proofs for decentralization. Most of the papers, blog posts, etc. I read don't emphasize their consensus models enough, and when they do, it's generally worse than it's marketed to be. With all the latex these authors write to make their papers seem more mathematically grounded, you'd think we'd see more rigourous proof of security sections on decentralized consensus. I personally find the space lacking.

Most layer 2 designers hold the decentralization of Bitcoin's consensus model as an implicit security assumption, which is fine, but I want simple front and center proofs about why their layer 2 constructions are safe. Being able to build a foundation for one's proof from Bitcoin's consensus model makes proving decentralization so much easier and I hope more people take the rigorous approach to proving decentralization.
